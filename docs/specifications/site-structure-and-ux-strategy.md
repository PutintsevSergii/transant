## 1. Executive recommendation

The website should position TransANT as an engineering partner that starts with the customer's transport task and delivers a lighter, adaptable freight-wagon solution. It should not behave like a corporate brochure or a gallery of similar-looking wagons.

The central customer journey is:

```text
transport task
  -> suitable wagon family or custom route
  -> operational and economic benefit
  -> specifications and evidence
  -> confidence in engineering and delivery
  -> conversation with a TransANT expert
```

The experience should answer five questions in this order:

1. What does TransANT offer?
2. Is there a suitable solution for my cargo and operation?
3. What measurable benefit could it create?
4. Can I trust the engineering, approvals, quality, and delivery model?
5. How do I start a technically useful conversation?

The site should feel precise, energetic, and engineered. It should use the supplied wagon imagery as the visual protagonist, technical data as proof, and restrained motion to explain modularity. The immutable logo remains a signature, not an animated design element.

## 3. Strategic positioning

### 3.1 Recommended position

> TransANT engineers lightweight freight wagons around the cargo, route, and handling process — combining standardized platforms, purpose-built structures, homologation, and managed delivery.

This position is stronger than describing TransANT only as a wagon manufacturer. It communicates the company's compact engineering role, modular concept, partner-based production model, and ability to solve a specific logistics problem.

### 3.2 Message hierarchy

Use messages in this order:

1. **Customer outcome:** carry more useful cargo and adapt the wagon to the actual transport task.
2. **Product mechanism:** optimized lightweight construction plus a platform/body architecture.
3. **Operational relevance:** cargo fit, loading and unloading, route class, speed, gauge, maintainability, and future adaptation.
4. **Proof:** model specifications, attributed figures, operating cases, certificates, and partners.
5. **Responsible impact:** fewer resources per transported tonne and an optional CO2-reduced steel route, with clear calculation boundaries.

### 3.3 Working brand promise

Retain the established line where approved:

> Less weight. More freight.

Add a precise explanatory line rather than another slogan:

> Lightweight and adaptable freight wagons engineered for your cargo, route, and handling process.

### 3.4 Voice

The copy should be:

- confident but not inflated;
- technically literate but understandable to commercial visitors;
- specific about conditions, configurations, and evidence;
- concise at the top of a page and increasingly detailed as the visitor scrolls;
- focused on customer work rather than internal self-description.

Prefer:

- “Up to 4 t additional payload in the documented configuration.”
- “Discuss your transport task with an engineer.”
- “Available for 20, 30, and 40 ft ISO containers.”

Avoid:

- repeated use of “revolutionary,” “perfect,” “green,” or “best”;
- universal claims derived from one model or one operating case;
- claims that confuse DAC-ready design with installed and certified equipment;
- sustainability statements without a defined lifecycle or calculation boundary.

## 5. Experience principles

### 5.1 Start with the task

Allow visitors to begin with what they carry, how it is handled, or which wagon type they already know. Do not force everyone to understand TransANT's internal product naming before they can find a solution.

### 5.2 Show the wagon before explaining the company

The product is the strongest visual and commercial evidence. A clear wagon render and concise outcome should appear before company history, values, or partner logos.

### 5.3 Pair every benefit with a mechanism or proof

Claims such as lower weight, more payload, flexibility, and lower impact should link directly to a specification, diagram, case study, certificate, or attributed source.

### 5.4 Layer complexity

Use this information depth model:

```text
scan:       name, cargo fit, image, 3–4 decisive metrics
understand: benefits, configuration, platform/body explanation
validate:   full specifications, load tables, approvals, downloads
act:        contextual enquiry with the product and task preselected
```

### 5.5 Keep two routes to conversion

Visitors should always have both:

- a product-led route: **Explore wagons**;
- a problem-led route: **Discuss a transport task**.

This prevents the catalogue from becoming a dead end when no standard model is an exact fit.

### 5.6 Make trust visible, not decorative

Certificates, operating cases, partner roles, and claim sources should be presented as readable evidence. Unexplained logo walls should not replace proof.

## 7. Global navigation

### 7.1 Desktop header

Recommended primary navigation:

```text
[immutable logo]  Wagons  Solutions  Technology  Proof  Company  Resources  [Talk to an engineer]
```

Utility controls:

- language selector;
- optional compact search only when the content volume justifies it;
- no social icons in the primary navigation.

Behavior:

- transparent or dark over the hero only when logo contrast is guaranteed;
- becomes a compact solid header after scrolling;
- the CTA remains visible but does not visually compete with the logo;
- current section is identified by text and shape, not color alone;
- the logo scales proportionally and never changes form between states.

### 7.2 Wagons mega menu

The Wagons menu should support two mental models:

```text
By cargo                         By wagon family
Containers and swap bodies      Intermodal
Steel and long cargo            Flat
Timber                           Timber
Scrap and bulk                  Open / Multi
Liquid bulk                     Tank

[Find the right wagon]          [View all wagons]
```

Include one small product render or engineering line drawing, not a carousel. The menu should remain useful with keyboard navigation and at 200% zoom.

### 7.3 Mobile navigation

- Open as a full-height panel with a visible close button and trapped focus.
- Show top-level destinations first.
- Expand Wagons and Proof as labelled accordions.
- Keep **Talk to an engineer** visible near the top and again at the bottom.
- Do not place essential destinations behind horizontal swiping.
- Preserve the user's current language and return focus to the menu trigger on close.

### 7.4 Footer

Organize the footer into four useful groups:

| Products      | Expertise      | Company        | Practical           |
| ------------- | -------------- | -------------- | ------------------- |
| All wagons    | Solutions      | About TransANT | Contact             |
| Five families | Technology     | Projects       | Downloads           |
| Find by cargo | Sustainability | Quality        | Imprint and privacy |

Add the legal company name, address, approved phone/email, language switcher, and LinkedIn link. Do not repeat a large marketing block in the footer.

## 8. Homepage blueprint

The homepage should tell one continuous story rather than act as a collection of unrelated campaign blocks.

### Section 1 — Hero: immediate category and value

**Visitor question:** What is this company and why should I care?

Recommended content:

- eyebrow: `Freight wagons for the European standard-gauge market`;
- H1: `Less weight. More freight.`;
- explanation: `Lightweight and adaptable freight wagons engineered for your cargo, route, and handling process.`;
- primary CTA: `Explore wagons`;
- secondary CTA: `Discuss your transport task`;
- one dominant wagon render or approved operational image;
- compact evidence line: `Engineering · Homologation · Managed production · Delivery`.

Visual behavior:

- use a dark graphite field, a pale engineering grid, and one red wagon as the focal object;
- reveal platform dimensions and key load points as the visitor scrolls;
- keep text readable before the effect initializes;
- never animate or distort the logo.

### Section 2 — Task selector: “What do you need to move?”

**Visitor question:** Is there a relevant solution for my work?

Use five large selectable cargo cards:

- containers and swap bodies;
- steel, construction, and long cargo;
- timber;
- scrap and bulk;
- liquid bulk.

Each selection immediately reveals suitable wagon families and one or two differentiating metrics. Provide `Not sure? Describe your transport task` as an equal path.

### Section 3 — Value system: lighter, adaptable, specific

**Visitor question:** What is different about TransANT?

Use three connected panels:

1. **Lightweight by engineering** — high-strength steel and topology optimization reduce unnecessary mass.
2. **Adaptable by architecture** — standardized platforms work with cargo-specific bodies and configurations.
3. **Specific to the operation** — cargo, route, loading, unloading, and approval requirements shape the solution.

Avoid three generic icon cards. Use an explanatory diagram that moves from platform to body to operational outcome.

### Section 4 — Supported performance

**Visitor question:** What could the benefit be?

Show only approved, bounded metrics. Initial candidates:

| Metric                                    | Display condition                                        |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Up to 20% lighter underframe              | Attribute to the documented comparison basis             |
| Up to 4 t additional payload per wagon    | Preserve “up to” and link to its model/case context      |
| 33–70 ft platform architecture            | Confirm that the full range remains commercially current |
| Up to 100 train journeys avoided annually | Show only inside the Erzberg–Linz case study             |

Every metric card shall include a visible `See basis` link. Do not place an unqualified number in a decorative counter.

### Section 5 — Wagon families

**Visitor question:** What can I buy or discuss today?

Display the five families in the V7 Wagon Switchyard: one large active wagon stage, one focused editorial summary, and a clear row of five direct family controls. Each family state contains:

- family name and cargo fit;
- one consistent wagon image;
- one family-specific operational advantage;
- model count based on approved launch data;
- `View family` action.

Do not auto-advance. All five family destinations must remain ordinary server-rendered links and readable without JavaScript. Do not add search, filtering, result counts, comparison controls, or configurator behaviour in the first release.

### Section 6 — Platform story

**Visitor question:** How does the concept work?

Use a scroll-guided but fully accessible assembly story:

```text
standardized platform
  + cargo-specific structure
  + engineering and homologation
  + managed production and quality
  = a wagon matched to the transport task
```

The animation should clarify the relationship between parts. It should not be a decorative explosion view without labels.

### Section 7 — Operating proof

**Visitor question:** Has the approach worked outside a render?

Lead with the strongest approved case, currently the BulkBox ore route. Use:

- one operational photograph;
- route and customer context;
- the problem;
- the wagon solution;
- bounded measured or attributed result;
- `Read project` link.

Secondary project cards may show the scrap prototypes and 60 ft Sgns production only after their current status and publication rights are confirmed.

### Section 8 — How collaboration works

**Visitor question:** What happens after I contact TransANT?

Present a six-stage process:

1. Transport task and requirements.
2. Platform, structure, and configuration.
3. Simulation and engineering.
4. Homologation and documentation.
5. Managed production and quality assurance.
6. Acceptance and handover.

Label service steps that still require scope confirmation. This section should reduce perceived procurement risk, not make an unsupported delivery promise.

### Section 9 — Quality and responsible impact

**Visitor question:** Can procurement and ESG teams validate the supplier?

Use two evidence-rich columns:

- **Quality:** ISO 9001:2015 and EN 15085-2 CL1 cards with scope, issuer, validity, and PDF.
- **Responsible impact:** lightweight operation, adaptability across lifecycle needs, and optional greentec steel, each with a defined evidence boundary.

Do not combine certificates, EcoVadis, CO2 figures, and partner logos into an unexplained badge wall.

### Section 10 — Final conversion

Recommended heading:

> Start with the cargo, not a catalogue number.

Supporting copy:

> Tell us what you transport, where it runs, and how it is handled. We will help identify a suitable wagon or the right engineering route.

Actions:

- primary: `Discuss your transport task`;
- secondary: `Download product information`;
- display a named sales or engineering contact only after role and contact details are confirmed.

## 9. Wagons catalogue UX

### 9.1 Catalogue landing page

The launch catalogue must support three tasks through direct content and navigation:

1. **I know the wagon type** — choose one of five clearly named families.
2. **I know the model** — scan the visible model names and wagon codes.
3. **I need a custom solution** — follow a clear engineering-enquiry route.

Recommended interface:

- concise page introduction;
- five editorial family rows in a stable source order;
- one consistent representative image, cargo fit, model links, and family action per row;
- visible direct links to all ten launch products;
- a custom-solution path after the family sequence.

Do not add catalogue search, chips, filters, result counts, no-results states, or a marketplace-style control layer in the first release. Ten products are faster to understand through direct browsing. Search and comparison remain later enhancements if catalogue volume grows.

### 9.2 Product card

Every product card should contain the same hierarchy:

```text
[wagon image]
family label
product name + wagon code
one-sentence cargo fit
3 decisive metrics
View specifications ->
```

Use meaningful family differences for the metrics. For example, tank capacity matters for Zacns, loading volume for Eanos, loading length for flat wagons, and pin configurations for Sgns.

### 9.3 Comparison

Product comparison is a future P1 enhancement and is not part of release one. If commissioned later, it should:

- compare no more than three products at once;
- align equivalent fields and clearly mark non-equivalent ones;
- allow differences-only view;
- keep units consistent;
- preserve all comparison data in HTML or accessible labels;
- offer `Discuss these options` with selected models prefilled in the enquiry.

## 10. Category page template

Each category page should include:

1. category name and cargo-oriented introduction;
2. category hero using one representative wagon;
3. suitability list: what it carries and how it is typically handled;
4. model grid with consistent metrics;
5. category-specific design features;
6. relevant operating proof or technical explanation;
7. related solution links;
8. contextual enquiry CTA.

Avoid duplicating the same “lightweight, flexible, sustainable” paragraph on all five pages. Each category needs a distinct operational narrative.

## 11. Product detail page template

### Above the fold

- breadcrumb;
- category label;
- precise product name and wagon code;
- short cargo-fit statement;
- large consistent wagon render;
- three or four decisive facts;
- primary CTA `Discuss this wagon`;
- secondary CTA `Download datasheet` when a real file exists.

### Recommended page sequence

```text
overview and decisive metrics
-> cargo fit
-> operational benefits
-> configuration/features
-> technical specifications
-> line-class load limits
-> approvals and availability status
-> downloads
-> related evidence/project
-> related wagons
-> contextual enquiry
```

### Specification interface

- Group values into dimensions, running characteristics, loading, equipment, and special features.
- Keep the complete table crawlable and accessible.
- Provide sticky section navigation on long desktop pages.
- On mobile, use labelled groups rather than an unreadably wide table.
- Explain abbreviations such as DAC, RID, TSI, GCU, UIC, G1, and G2 in a glossary or inline definition.
- Show source/verification status internally; expose public attribution where it materially supports a claim.

### Contextual enquiry

Selecting `Discuss this wagon` should pass the product ID and locale to the contact form. The visitor should not need to type the product name again.

## 12. Solutions page

The Solutions page translates the product catalogue into the buyer's language.

For each cargo group, show:

- cargo and handling challenge;
- relevant wagon families;
- configuration considerations;
- decisive specifications;
- an approved operational example when available;
- CTA to compare wagons or describe the task.

Do not create thin SEO pages that merely repeat category descriptions. A solution page is useful only when it adds decision guidance.

## 13. Technology page

The Technology page should explain why the benefit exists.

Recommended narrative:

1. **Remove unnecessary mass** — topology optimization and high-strength steel.
2. **Standardize the foundation** — platform lengths and common components.
3. **Adapt the transport structure** — industry-specific or customer-specific bodies.
4. **Engineer for the operation** — cargo, terminal, route, and securing requirements.
5. **Validate and deliver** — simulation, homologation, quality, and partner production.
6. **Prepare for change** — interchangeable concepts and DAC readiness where model-specific evidence supports it.

Use labelled diagrams, section cuts, and engineering overlays. Avoid showing proprietary drawings at a resolution that exposes protected details unless approved.

## 14. Projects and evidence

### 14.1 Project index

Project cards should state:

- project or route;
- cargo;
- wagon/configuration;
- operational status;
- one verified outcome;
- publication date and latest verification date.

### 14.2 Case-study template

```text
context
-> transport problem
-> design constraints
-> TransANT solution
-> engineering and delivery process
-> measured/attributed result
-> current operating status
-> customer quotation if approved
-> related wagon and enquiry
```

The BulkBox case is the best initial flagship because it has public operating context. The scrap-wagon story should be labelled as testing until final results are approved. The 60 ft Sgns production story should distinguish manufacture, technical acceptance, delivery, and operational service.

## 15. Quality and certificates

The Quality page should serve procurement, engineering, and compliance visitors.

Each certificate card shall show:

- standard and certificate name;
- certified legal entity;
- scope;
- classification where applicable;
- issuer;
- issue and validity dates;
- direct PDF download;
- latest verification date.

Current public candidates include ISO 9001:2015 for development, homologation, and distribution of freight and tank wagons, and EN 15085-2 CL1 for railway vehicle/component welding activities. The source PDFs remain the authority.

Add a concise quality process explaining requirements control, engineering review, supplier quality, production oversight, acceptance, and handover only after the actual responsibility boundary is confirmed.

## 16. Sustainability page (retired)

Sustainability is not a public route or navigation destination. Do not publish the earlier environmental, greentec, or EcoVadis narrative. Quality and certificate evidence remains on `/quality`; wagon information comes from the supplied catalogue.

## 17. Company page

The Company page should establish capability and accountability, not begin with abstract mission text.

Recommended sequence:

1. concise description of TransAnt GmbH and its role;
2. engineering and delivery capabilities;
3. partner model and clearly described responsibilities;
4. company milestones from concept to current production activity;
5. management and team, using current approved information;
6. location and contact;
7. careers/LinkedIn link if no careers system exists.

Use partner logos only with supplied files and documented permissions. Do not publish ownership percentages or employee counts without current confirmation.

## 18. Downloads

Organize downloads by task, not one undifferentiated list:

- product datasheets;
- certificates;
- company and capability documents;
- sustainability evidence;
- media files, only if approved.

Each item should show title, language, file type, file size, revision date, and applicable model. Do not offer an empty download button or collect personal data merely to access public certificates.

## 19. Contact and conversion design

### 19.1 Primary conversion

The primary conversion is a qualified technical conversation, not an anonymous generic lead.

Recommended CTA label:

> Talk to an engineer

Alternative contextual labels:

- `Discuss this wagon`;
- `Describe your transport task`;
- `Ask about this configuration`.

### 19.2 Form design

Keep the first interaction manageable while collecting useful context.

Required fields:

- name;
- company;
- business email;
- message or transport task;
- privacy acknowledgement.

Useful optional or conditional fields:

- selected product/family, prefilled from context;
- cargo type;
- origin/destination market or operating region;
- annual volume or expected fleet quantity;
- loading/unloading method;
- desired timeline;
- phone.

Use a single page with progressive disclosure rather than a long multi-step form. Show a human-readable response expectation only after TransANT confirms an achievable service level.

### 19.3 Mail-client handoff

The prepared-email state should:

- state that the visitor's mail application should open with the enquiry prepared;
- tell the visitor to review and send the message from that application;
- never imply that the website has sent, received, accepted, or stored the enquiry;
- retain the entered values so the visitor can verify or retry the handoff.

## 20. Visual direction

### 20.1 Creative concept: Engineered movement

The visual language should combine the rigor of an engineering drawing with the physical presence of rail equipment.

Key ingredients:

- dark graphite sections as structural anchors;
- pale paper and steel-blue surfaces for readable technical content;
- red used for the wagon, primary action, and decisive benefit;
- cyan/blue used for specifications, diagrams, and evidence;
- large product silhouettes with generous clear space;
- thin measurement lines, axle markers, route lines, and modular grids;
- controlled asymmetry inspired by platform lengths and wagon geometry.

The supplied imagery supports two complementary modes:

1. **Product precision:** isolated red wagon renders and technical drawings.
2. **Operational credibility:** real rail environments, loading contexts, and production evidence.

The red-lit reflection artwork can inspire campaign moments but should not become the default background of every product page. The operational train image includes competing visual subjects and requires careful cropping and contextual use; it is better suited to a project or company story than a universal hero.

### 20.2 Typography

Recommended working direction, subject to a design proof:

- a contemporary grotesk such as Manrope for headings and interface text;
- IBM Plex Mono or an equivalent technical monospace for model codes, measurements, and evidence labels;
- tabular numerals for specifications and comparison tables;
- restrained uppercase for short labels only.

The typography shall not imitate or re-create the logo wordmark. Fonts should be self-hosted when their licenses permit it.

### 20.3 Layout rhythm

- Use a 12-column desktop grid and content-led container grids.
- Alternate immersive product moments with compact evidence sections.
- Keep body-copy measure near 60–75 characters.
- Use large headings sparingly and never let display type displace decisive product information below the fold.
- Treat specification tables as first-class designed components, not default browser tables.

### 20.4 Photography and renders

- Use one dominant visual idea per section.
- Do not mix render angles inside a product comparison grid.
- Separate isolated renders from operational photography through background and caption treatment.
- Caption operational images with location/project/status where approved.
- Avoid generic stock photography of trains when authentic client material exists.

## 21. Motion direction

Motion should explain engineering relationships and guide attention.

Recommended signature moments:

- hero wagon enters along a horizontal rail axis while measurement lines resolve;
- a platform/body explanation assembles in labelled stages;
- product-family changes use a restrained lateral transition that preserves orientation;
- specifications reveal by group without delaying access;
- page transitions echo continuous rail movement using browser View Transitions where supported.

Avoid:

- animating the logo;
- perpetual parallax;
- scroll hijacking;
- long intro sequences;
- motion that conceals content until the visitor scrolls precisely;
- gratuitous 3D before the product catalogue and enquiry journey are complete.

Every effect requires a reduced-motion equivalent. Mobile motion should be shorter and simpler than desktop motion.

| Level | Meaning                                                                   | Public treatment                    |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| E1    | Current certificate, official technical sheet, or approved company record | May be stated directly with source  |
| E2    | Documented operating case with defined context                            | State with case conditions and date |
| E3    | Attributed partner or company claim                                       | Preserve attribution and qualifiers |
| E4    | Unconfirmed, outdated, or internally supplied without approval            | Do not publish                      |

The CMS/data model should retain evidence level, source URL/file, checked date, owner, and publication status. Visual `See basis` links should be available for important metrics without making every sentence look academic.

## 24. Search, SEO, and findability

- Use model names and wagon codes in page titles, headings, URLs, and internal links.
- Connect cargo synonyms to the right categories: scrap, recyclables, ore, aggregates, timber, roundwood, ISO containers, swap bodies, steel, rails, chemicals, and petroleum products.
- Provide breadcrumb navigation on category, product, project, and resource pages.
- Link every solution to products and every product back to relevant solutions and evidence.
- Use onsite search only when insights and downloads make browsing insufficient; ten launch products do not justify a complex search system.
- Generate localized canonical and `hreflang` metadata only for pages with approved equivalents.
- Avoid indexing filter combinations unless a deliberate SEO landing page contains unique decision content.

## 25. Measurement plan

Track useful business and UX events with privacy approval:

- `cargo_selector_used`;
- `product_viewed`;
- `specifications_opened`;
- `comparison_started` and `comparison_completed` only after the future comparison feature is commissioned;
- `datasheet_downloaded`;
- `contact_cta_selected` with page/product context;
- `form_started`, `form_error`, and `form_submitted`;
- locale changes and no-results states.

Primary indicators:

- qualified enquiries containing a product or transport task;
- product-to-enquiry conversion;
- successful datasheet access;
- task success for finding a suitable wagon;
- form completion and error rate;
- organic entry traffic to category and product pages.

Do not optimize for raw time on site. A technical buyer who finds a specification quickly and contacts the company is a better outcome than a visitor forced through a long presentation.

## 28. Validation before design lock

Test the proposed structure with at least one representative from sales, engineering, and an external buyer profile.

Priority usability tasks:

1. Find a wagon for 40 ft ISO containers and identify its tare weight.
2. Find the most relevant options for scrap transport and compare their volume.
3. Determine whether a timber wagon supports the required log lengths.
4. Find the certification scope and download the certificate.
5. Understand what “modular” means without reading the entire Technology page.
6. Locate the basis for the “up to 4 t” benefit.
7. Start an enquiry from a product page without re-entering the product.
8. Complete the same tasks with keyboard navigation and on a 375 px mobile viewport.

The structure is ready for production design when participants can complete these tasks without relying on facilitator explanation, and when sales and engineering agree that the resulting enquiries contain useful context.

## 30. Final design rule

The new website should make a technical buyer feel three things in sequence:

1. **Relevance:** “They understand my transport problem.”
2. **Confidence:** “The product logic and evidence are credible.”
3. **Momentum:** “I know exactly what to inspect next or whom to contact.”

Every page, component, animation, and CTA should support at least one of those outcomes. If it supports none of them, it should not be in the first release.
